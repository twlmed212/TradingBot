## Method

- Lockers scanned: **Streamflow** and **Jupiter Lock**, via Helius `getProgramAccounts` filtered by mint. Field offsets were validated on KMNO (100M KMNO over 2 years hourly = 5,708 per hour, as decoded) and JTO.
- A schedule written into a contract is public from the contract's creation, so unlocks are "known at time t" only if the contract existed before t. Streamflow stores `created_at`; for Jupiter Lock the first transaction time is used (looked up for large contracts only).
- Large contract: ≥ 0.1% of today's total supply. Big-unlock window: releases from large contracts totalling ≥ 2% of today's total supply within 14 days (non-overlapping windows). Research window: 1 Jun 2023 – 30 Jun 2025. *Provable* = every contributing contract existed before the window started.

## What this means for H4

- Tokens with at least one provable big-unlock window in the research window: **0** (none), 0 windows in total.
- Tokens with no large on-chain contracts are not 'no unlock' tokens: their team/investor vesting is likely held by custodians or multisigs, which this scan cannot see. They must be treated as *unknown*, never as zero unlocks.
- Survivorship: contracts closed after vesting are not returned by `getProgramAccounts`, so older unlocks can be missing. Supply shares use today's total supply, not historical circulating supply, which understates shares for tokens whose supply grew and overstates them where supply was burned.

## Decision input

A historical H4 test is only meaningful if the provable sample above is broad enough (the registration must set a minimum, e.g. several tokens and tens of windows). If it is not, the honest options are forward-only collection (archive schedules as they are published and evaluate later) or a paid unlock feed that can prove publication dates. Either way, unknown coverage stays unknown.
